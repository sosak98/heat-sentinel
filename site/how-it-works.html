The pipeline

From street-level reading to field action — four stages, 24/7

HeatSentinel is not a dashboard: it is an agent. It observes, predicts, decides and acts autonomously, with every decision written to an audit ledger.

1

Observe — what is happening at street level, right now

The agent continuously reads the 20 mesh points (temperature modeled 2 m above ground, 20 m² resolution). It also validates every reading: is this temperature normal for this hour? A 48-hour z-score (|z| ≥ 2.5) flags local micro-spikes and sensor drift in real time.

Example: at 14:00, the port district reads 41 °C where the 48 h profile expects 38 °C → anomaly raised before any alert decision.
2

Predict — the peak six hours ahead, point by point

A 2 MB hybrid model (Ridge trend component + LightGBM) nowcasts each point's temperature peak over the next six hours. Six hours is the actionable horizon: early enough to open a cooling center or move outdoor work, recent enough to be accurate. Measured precision: MAE 0.61 °C on real Phoenix data, 0.26 °C on the Cotonou configuration (calibrated against real weather).

19 interpretable features: 24/48/72 h lags, 6/12/24/72 h trends, diurnal phase, per-zone UHI coefficient, wet-bulb temperature (Stull 2011) as the felt-heat proxy.
3

Decide — transparent rules, no black box

When the forecast crosses a risk threshold (score ≥ 45/100, or an anomaly), the agent prepares an alert. Two safeguards: it never re-alerts the same zone within 6 hours (no spam), and it only escalates levels, never de-escalates. No LLM in the safety loop — a system that protects people must be fully explainable. Every decision lands in a JSONL audit ledger.

Ledger: « 14:02 · Garfield Ctr · score 82 → escalate Vigilance → Critical · alert #4 dispatched »
4

Act — the alert goes out with the right next steps

Alerts are dispatched by SMS or WhatsApp (Twilio), localized per city, and they do not just say "it is hot" — they say what to do. Phoenix demo: English. Cotonou deployment: French + Fon, the language of the most exposed southern districts.

🧮

The risk score (0–100), fully transparent

The score blends the predicted peak (65% weight) with humidity (35% — the driver of "feels-like"). Thresholds follow WHO heat-plan bands: green <30 · yellow <50 · orange <70 · red <85 · black ≥85, recalibrated per climate (Phoenix 35–45 °C, Cotonou 28–38 °C). Anyone can verify the arithmetic: the formula is public in the repo.

🔌

What happens when the grid goes down

Extreme heat degrades power and connectivity — the system is edge-first: the model runs locally on an NVIDIA Jetson ($120, 8 GB), <10 ms inference, no internet required. During outages, monitoring and local alerts continue; the audit ledger syncs when the network returns. This is the edge–cloud architecture deployed in NVIDIA AI Factories.

Where the AI is used — and where it deliberately isn't

Predictive where it is hard, deterministic where it is vital

📈

AI for prediction

The 6-hour nowcast is the genuine ML problem: 19 interpretable signals combined by a Ridge + LightGBM hybrid, trained with a strict temporal hold-out (no leakage). Trees extrapolate poorly at the top of a heat-wave ramp — the Ridge trend component fixes exactly that.

🧾

Rules for decisions

The alert decision is never delegated to a stochastic model. In a safety-critical loop, transparency beats sophistication: a city engineer must be able to read why the system acted — and it can be read, line by line, in the ledger.

🤝

Humans in charge

Every alert names actions that humans execute: hospitals, schools, markets. The AI does not replace anyone — it delivers the six-hour head start, the right message, in the right language, at the right time.
