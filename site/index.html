Heat kills at street level.
We see it coming six hours early.

HeatSentinel is an agentic AI system that turns the FortyGuard Temperature API® (temperatures measured two meters above ground at 20 m² resolution) into actionable early warnings for hospitals, schools and outdoor workers. This demo runs on real, live mesh data from Phoenix, 42.9 °C real street-level peak (Downtown, Aug 26) during an ongoing heat wave.

The problem

The deadliest climate hazard is the least prepared for, and cities still manage it with yesterday's tools

🌫️

Coarse data

Official forecasts describe a city from an airport sensor 10 km away. A 2–4 °C difference between a port district and a beach block is exactly the difference between a workday and a heat stroke, and it is invisible at that scale.

⏰

Late warnings

Heat bulletins arrive at 8 a.m.; the peak lands at 3 p.m. The window to open a cooling center, pause outdoor work or staff the ER is already half gone.

No action loop

Even when a warning arrives, nobody owns the next step: who opens the cooling center? Who escalates the hospital? Who notifies outdoor crews, in the language they speak?

The solution

An AI agent that watches, predicts, decides and acts: from reading to action in 60 seconds

HeatSentinel closes the full loop on 20 measurement points of the Temperature API® mesh (20 m² resolution, modeled 2 m above ground, FortyGuard's mesh; no hardware installed by us).

👁️

Observe

20 street-level points, continuously, with anomaly detection (48 h z-score) that flags local spikes and sensor drift as they happen.

🔮

Predict

The temperature peak six hours ahead, per point, with a 2 MB hybrid model (MAE 0.36 °C on real Phoenix data).

⚖️

Decide

A transparent rule engine (no black box, no LLM in the safety loop) that escalates only when needed and never spams.

📲

Act

Localized alerts by SMS / WhatsApp with concrete actions: open cooling centers, ER standby, pause outdoor work 14:00–17:00.

Not a simulation: proven on real mesh data, during a real heat wave

FortyGuard's trial key let us do what most entries cannot: harvest real data. The mesh does not yet cover West Africa, so we demonstrated the system on Phoenix, one of the hottest cities in the mesh, through three full days of an active heat wave (Aug 24–26). Every day of real data re-anchors the model; the same pipeline drops onto Cotonou, our deployment target, the day coverage arrives.

Built for the edge, because grids go down in heat waves

The model ships at 2 MB, infers in <10 ms and runs on an NVIDIA Jetson with no internet. When power and connectivity fail (the usual pattern during extreme heat), local monitoring and alerts keep running. The winning Jetson kit would become our first deployed node.

🎯

City-agnostic by design

One codebase, any city: climate, thresholds and alert language are configured per city (Phoenix: dry heat, English alerts · Cotonou: humid heat, French alerts). Phoenix proves the stack on real data; Cotonou, our home city, ~700,000 residents, feels-like >40 °C, is where it goes first.
