The team

ClimVision: built in Cotonou, proven on real data, for any city

We are a two-person team from Cotonou, Benin. We do not study heat from ten thousand kilometers away: we live in it. Cotonou (~700,000 residents in the commune, 1.5M+ in the metro) is our deployment target. Phoenix is where the mesh lets us prove the system today, on real data.

Why Cotonou, why us

Humid tropical heat does what the forecast doesn't tell you: with the humidity, the feels-like crosses 40 °C while the thermometer reads 32 °C. There is currently no hyperlocal early-warning system in the city. The 20 mesh points were chosen for that reality: the port and industrial zone, dense districts without shade, the beach where the sea breeze saves people, mixed quarters in between.

📍

Local knowledge, encoded

Alerts go out in French, the language of the most exposed districts, because a 3 p.m. alert in English at the Dantokpa market protects nobody. The same system, configured for Phoenix, alerts in English. Localization is a configuration, not a fork.

⚡

Built for real conditions

Power cuts, unstable internet, no data center: the system is designed to run on a Jetson-class edge device (<$250), offline, at street level. Not for a showcase in an air-conditioned room.

🤝

A civic tool, not a black box

Transparent decisions, a public audit ledger, humans in charge of every action. Heat resilience as infrastructure, owned and readable by the city it protects.

After the hackathon

The ask

🏙️ A 30-day pilot with the City of Cotonou

Five priority sites: the CHU (regional hospital), three schools, and the port market, with rehearsed response protocols tied to our alert levels. We provide the system; the city provides the five people who execute the actions.

🔑 Temperature API scale credits + the Jetson kit

API capacity for the pilot once Cotonou is covered, and the winning NVIDIA Jetson kit as our first deployed edge node, the agent running locally on the kit, self-calibrating against the Temperature API®.
